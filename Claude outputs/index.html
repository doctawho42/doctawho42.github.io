Metabolite Predictors Have a Multiverse Problem

A rule-grounded predictor for metabolite structures, and what happens to every published comparison once the undeclared choices are declared.

N. L. Polomoshnov, A. V. Rudik (IBMC) · manuscript, J. Chem. Inf. Model.

Comparing two metabolite predictors means choosing when two structures count as the same molecule, how long a candidate list is allowed to be, and which substrates get scored. None of those choices is declared in this literature, and none of them is decorative: varying the structure-matching criterion alone moves the verdict at 6 of 9 output budgets. Reported as a grid rather than as one number, most of the advantages this field prints stop being advantages.

Abstract

GRAIL is a rule-grounded metabolite-structure predictor: a bank of 7,581 reaction templates spanning 4,417 transformation types is applied to a substrate, and the candidates are ranked by reciprocal rank fusion. Because the system is rules plus a ranking, recall@k factorises exactly into three terms — what the bank can produce at all, what survives selection, and what the ranking puts inside the budget — so a single recall number can be decomposed into the part that is a ranking failure and the part that is not.

On the comparison set the bank ceiling is 0.8105 and the exhaustive configuration reaches 0.7023 at k = 50, which is 86.7% of what the bank makes achievable at all. Of the shortfall, 0.1082 is within reach of a better ranking and 0.1895 is not, because the bank never produces those structures. Of the 475 uncovered references, 337 belong to a transformation type the bank does not contain, 98 come from a rule that exists but does not fire, and 40 cannot be typed. Between 0.6253 and 0.7095 of the shortfall is chemistry the training corpus never contained. The bottleneck is the bank, not the ranking, and effort spent on ranking cannot reach most of what is missing.

The comparison against four published predictors is reported as a grid over declared evaluation choices rather than as a single table row, and the grid is what produces the title.

What this is not

This is not a claim that GRAIL is the best metabolite predictor. Read under a declared grid, most of its apparent advantages do not survive. Against GLORYx at matched list length the difference is +0.0135 [−0.0217, +0.0491] and the interval contains zero; the interactive configuration is worse than GLORYx, at −0.1203 [−0.1685, −0.0754]. The one advantage that survives both the multiplicity correction and the control for list length is +0.1820 [+0.1446, +0.2208] over SyGMa. A comparison that looks decisive at a fixed k can disappear once the two systems are made to emit lists of the same length, and list length is not something this literature reports.

It is also not an independent rule bank. Of the 1,233 curated templates, 966 (78.3%) are taken verbatim from other people's work: 611 from BioTransformer, 273 from SyGMa, 82 from GLORYx. The released bank contains 6,970 of the 7,581 templates; the 611 BioTransformer-derived templates are withheld for licence reasons, and provenance for every template is recorded in NOTICE.md.

And the training corpus is not clean. It draws on four public sources, and the selection that produced it is not recoverable from any record. Comparing the MetXBioDB pairs that entered the corpus against those that did not, their transformation-type distributions differ by a total variation distance of 0.317, a permutation test over 10,000 shuffles puts the chance of a distance that large at p = 0.0001, and 156 of the dropped pairs carry a transformation type the kept half never shows. Whoever assembled it was not sampling blind to chemistry, and the filter is documented nowhere.

Cost

Two configurations are reported because they are different products, not two settings of one. The interactive arm applies 30 rules, returns a median 15.6 candidates and takes 0.39 s; the exhaustive arm applies 7,580 rules, returns 98.1 candidates and takes 5.28 s. The exhaustive arm is the one that competes; the interactive arm is the one that can sit behind a web form. Reporting only the first would overstate what the deployed service does.

Data

The corpus splits 9,011 / 1,020 / 1,198. The evaluated test set is 1,170 substrates carrying 2,597 reference metabolites; the comparison set, on which every predictor is scored on the same substrates, is 291 substrates and 665 references. Candidate pools are capped at 100 and fused with reciprocal rank fusion at K = 60. Code and the released rule bank are at github.com/doctawho42/GRAIL, under GPL-3 and LGPL as the provenance of each component requires.
