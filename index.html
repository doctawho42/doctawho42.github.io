I work on one question that keeps recurring across unrelated sciences: when a model reports a quantity, is the uncertainty attached to it a usable object or only a plausible-looking one? The method is the same each time — take something the field reports as a single number, prove that it splits exactly, measure which term binds, and publish honestly where my own intervention failed. Two of my three manuscripts report a pre-registered negative result as their central finding.

Ground Truth That Does Not Ground: substituting reference σ-profiles degrades COSMO-SAC solubility prediction

with A. V. Rudik · under review, J. Chem. Inf. Model.

A graph network trains through a fixed COSMO-SAC layer, made differentiable, and an exact decomposition separates the fixed map's own misspecification from what its inputs fail to carry. Substituting the tabulated reference profile at inference makes prediction worse at every seed — grounding names two operations, and only one of them has a measured sign.

What Cycle Closure Can and Cannot See in a Relative Binding Free Energy Network

sole author · in preparation (J. Chem. Theory Comput.)

The per-edge BAR uncertainty a free-energy protocol already reports, once checked against replicate scatter on 1145 public OpenFE binding edges, is calibrated well enough to serve as the null of a cycle-closure goodness-of-fit test. A gradient–cycle decomposition then fixes exactly what such a test can and cannot see: the per-edge signal reproduces across replicates; a thresholded list of “bad” systems does not.
